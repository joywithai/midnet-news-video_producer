# English Full News
U.S. intelligence officials say Iran has substantially increased efforts to target American personnel, and that lethal plotting efforts have reached “advanced stages.” That warning appears in an unclassified October 23 memo from Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to Secretary of State Marco Rubio. Clayton said more State Department personnel may need access to the intelligence and offered to downgrade as much of it as possible so Rubio could share it more widely. The memo does not identify specific targets, describe the plots, or name countries outside the Middle East.

The warning comes amid a separate drawdown of U.S. diplomatic staff. The State Department ordered non-emergency employees and their families to leave U.S. posts in Saudi Arabia, including the embassy in Riyadh and consulates in Jeddah and Dhahran. Separately, officials said staffing would be temporarily reduced at U.S. missions in the United Arab Emirates, Lebanon, Qatar and Jordan under restricted operations. Those missions remain open; staff there were not given the same Saudi-style departure order. CNN reported that officials said there were no staffing changes in Bahrain or Kuwait.

The security measures also come as Iran-backed Houthi attacks have raised concerns about further regional escalation. The State Department updated travel advisories for countries in the region, but an advisory is not the same as an evacuation order. Iran has not publicly responded to the intelligence memo. Reports about possible targets remain allegations; the memo itself gives no target names or operational details.

President Donald Trump has publicly discussed the possibility of renewed military action against Iran or a deal, but no final decision has been announced. CNN reported that Israel had lobbied Washington for more immediate action, so far without success. With U.S. midterm elections approaching on November third, the question is how the administration will balance deterrence, diplomacy and the safety of personnel abroad. We will continue to follow verified developments.
